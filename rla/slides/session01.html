Part 1: Reading for meaning

You read a passage. Then you answer questions like these:

  • Order: What happened first, next and last?
  • Main idea: What is the most important point?
  • Inference: What does the text show, but not say?
  • Purpose and tone: Why did the author write it? How does the author feel?
  • Compare: How are two texts on one topic different?
The GED Academy · Ms. Maria7 / 22

Session 1 · GED RLA Introduction & Diagnostic

Part 2: Arguments and evidence

You read an argument. Then you judge it.

  • Claim: What does the author want you to believe?
  • Evidence: What proof does the author give?
  • Strong or weak: Is the proof on topic, and is there enough of it?
  • Graphs and data: How does a chart support the claim?
  • Two arguments: Which one has better evidence?

Never leave a blank

Common mistakeLeaving a question with no answer. A blank answer is always wrong.
  1. Remove the answers that cannot be right.
  2. Choose the best answer that is left.
  3. Go on to the next question.

What you write

One essay. 45 minutes.

  1. Read two passages with two different arguments.
  2. Decide which argument has more convincing evidence.
  3. Explain why. Use evidence from both passages.
