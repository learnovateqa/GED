The golden rule

Remember thisDo not choose the argument you agree with. Choose the argument the evidence supports.
X Weak answerI chose B because I hate school uniforms.
✓ Strong answerI chose A because it uses a three-year study, and B uses one family story.

The 4-Test Evidence Check

TestAsk
RelevantDoes it prove this exact claim?
CredibleIs the source trustworthy and fair?
SpecificDoes it give numbers, dates, names?
LogicalDoes it really lead to the claim?
TipIf evidence fails one test, it becomes weak.

Two myths

Common mistakeMyth 1: numbers are always strong. A number can be old, off topic or misleading.
Common mistakeMyth 2: examples are always weak. A clear example that fits the claim can be strong.

True does not mean relevant

✓ Strong relevant evidenceA city study: teenagers who slept under 7 hours scored lower in maths.

An opinion can be strong

Every claim is an opinion. It becomes a strong argument when facts support it.

X Weak argumentSchools should require uniforms because normal clothes are ugly.Opinion + opinion.
✓ Strong argumentSchools should require uniforms because discipline problems fell by 28% in schools that tried them.Opinion + fact.

Speed drill: 5 minutes

Two passagesPassage X: City parks help local shops. A 2023 business report found that shops near Central Park sold 22% more on park event days.

Passage Y: City parks are wonderful because my family loves picnics, and parks make everyone happy.
  1. What is the claim of each passage?
  2. Which is better supported?
  3. Give one reason from the text.
