Independent or dependent?

✓ Strong independent clauseThe bus arrived late.Subject + verb + complete thought. It can stand alone.
X Weak dependent clauseBecause the bus arrived late…Subject + verb, but the thought is not finished. Alone, it is a fragment.

The 3-question sentence test

  1. Is there a subject? (Who or what?)
  2. Is there a verb? (An -ing word alone does not count.)
  3. Is the thought complete? (Am I still waiting?)
Common mistakeJudging by length. A 24-word group can be a fragment. "She left." is a full sentence.

Run-on and comma splice

X Weak run-on (nothing between)The shop closed early we went home.
X Weak comma splice (only a comma)The shop closed early, we went home.
TipFinger test: cover the comma. If both sides are full sentences, a comma alone is wrong.

The four legal fixes

FixExample
1 · PeriodThe shop closed early. We went home.
2 · SemicolonThe shop closed early; we went home.
3 · Comma + FANBOYSThe shop closed early, so we went home.
4 · Waiting wordBecause the shop closed early, we went home.
TipFANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.

Think aloud

Which is the best revision?Many employees prefer flexible hours, they say it reduces stress.
  • A. …flexible hours they say it reduces stress.
  • B. …flexible hours; they say it reduces stress.
  • C. …flexible hours, and, they say it reduces stress.
  • D. …flexible hours, they say, it reduces stress.
✓ Strong answerB. A semicolon joins two complete, closely related sentences.

Subject–verb agreement

  1. Find the verb.
  2. Ask who or what does it. That is the subject.
  3. Cross out the words in the middle (of…, with…, along with…).
  4. Read subject + verb alone.
✓ Strong sentenceThe box of old photos and letters was in the cupboard.

Agreement rules

RuleExample
each, every, everyone, nobody = oneEveryone is ready.
A and B = more than oneThe manager and the clerk are here.
either…or / neither…nor: the nearer oneNeither the boxes nor the crate fits.
along with, as well as: no changeThe teacher, along with her students, was late.
team, staff, family = oneThe committee meets monthly.
there is / there are: subject after the verbThere are three reasons.

Session 5 · Sentence Structure & Grammar

Common GED traps

  • Long, so it must be a sentence. (No.)
  • A comma where your ear wants a pause.
  • A plural noun just before the verb: the box of letters were…
  • An -ing word as the main verb.
  • Fixing a splice with however and a comma. (However needs a semicolon or a period.)
  • Changing a sentence that is already correct.
