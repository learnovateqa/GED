What is an argument?

An argument is not a fight. It is writing that tries to make you believe or do something.

ReportArgument
It rained 5 cm yesterday.The city must fix the drains before the next storm.
gives factstakes a side and gives reasons

Fact or opinion?

  • Fact: you can check it. *The bus fare is 3 riyals.*
  • Opinion: a belief or feeling. *The bus is the best way to travel.*
TipWords like should, must, best, worst, unfair, essential often show an opinion or a claim.

The claim

The claim is the main point the author wants you to accept.

  • It is often in the first paragraph.
  • It is often repeated in the last paragraph.
  • Sometimes it is not written directly. Then you infer it.
✓ Strong claimSchools should give every student a free breakfast.

Session 3 · Analyzing Arguments

Answering the other side

A strong author shows the other side, then answers it.

ExampleSome people say free breakfast costs too much. However, hungry students learn less, and that costs more in the end.
TipSignal words: some people say, critics argue, however, on the other hand, while it is true that.

Session 3 · Analyzing Arguments

Hidden beliefs (assumptions)

An assumption is something the author believes but does not say.

ExampleWe should build a new car park, because more parking will bring more shoppers to the market.

Hidden belief: shoppers stay away because there is not enough parking. Is that true? The author does not prove it.
